# IMPOSSIBILITY_THEOREM — non-identifiability of the oracle query value from label-blind information

Development data only (old 405 cases; 80 states of the committed M3-margin paths). No new-pool label or input was accessed. Code: `code/twin_worlds.py`, `code/twin_worlds_analysis.py`; results: `results/twin_worlds_*.{csv,json}`. The derivations (§0–§3) were completed before the literature check (§K); the empirical section (§E) uses the old development labels only as the hidden truth of the constructed worlds.

---

## §0 Setting and notation

**Pool and labels.** A finite pool $U$ of inputs with features $x_u\in\mathbb R^4$ and metadata $z_u$. The simulator is deterministic, so the labels $y\in\{0,1\}^U$ are a fixed but unknown vector. At stage $n$ the revealed set is $D_n\subset U$ with labels $y_{D_n}$. The reference set $R\subseteq U\setminus D_n$ is the set of rows on which the endpoint is evaluated (in the thesis: the 17 Fold-B1-q20 rows of the outer test fold); the queriable set is $Q=U\setminus(D_n\cup R)$ (the unrevealed training pool). In the frozen protocol $R\cap Q=\emptyset$ and no query ever reveals a label in $R$.

**Label-blind information.** $I_n := (\{x_u,z_u\}_{u\in U},\ D_n,\ y_{D_n})$. Everything computed from $I_n$ is *label-blind*: every model fitted on the revealed labels (M3's Stage-1 threshold and Stage-2 discrepancy GP, the auxiliary T posterior, any committee), every posterior $p_n(u)$, latent moments, margins, densities, distances, and — importantly — the *flip sets* defined below for each hypothetical label. A **label-blind acquisition** is any deterministic map $A: I_n\mapsto x_A\in Q$. The set of hidden labelings compatible with $I_n$ is $\Omega_n=\{0,1\}^{U\setminus D_n}$ (nothing in $I_n$ constrains an unrevealed label); with a structural class $\mathcal H$ it is $\Omega_n(\mathcal H)=\{\omega:(y_{D_n},\omega)\in\mathcal H\}$ (Assumption A5 below).

**Learner, decisions, flips.** A learner $L$ maps a labelled set to a decision rule; $\hat y_n=L(D_n)$ and $\hat y_n^{x,y}=L(D_n\cup\{(x,y)\})$ for a hypothetical query $(x,y)$. The **flip set** of $x$ under label $y$ on $R$ is
$$F_y(x):=\{u\in R:\ \hat y_n^{x,y}(u)\neq\hat y_n(u)\},\qquad y\in\{0,1\}.$$
Both $F_0(x)$ and $F_1(x)$ are $I_n$-measurable (they are computed by refitting on revealed labels plus a hypothetical label; this is exactly what `inventions.py` computed as $I_0,I_1$ on the pool and `twin_worlds.py` computes on $R$).

**Correctness signs.** $e_u:=+1$ if $\hat y_n(u)=y_u$ and $-1$ otherwise, $u\in R$. The vector $e=(e_u)_{u\in R}$ is a deterministic function of the hidden $y_R$ given $I_n$; it is **not** $I_n$-measurable.

**Oracle value.** For the realised label $y_x$ of a candidate $x\in Q$,
$$V(x;\omega):=\mathrm{acc}_R(\hat y_n^{x,y_x})-\mathrm{acc}_R(\hat y_n)= -\frac{1}{|R|}\sum_{u\in F_{y_x}(x)} e_u .\tag{0.1}$$
(0.1) is an identity, not a model: a flipped row changes the accuracy by $-e_u/|R|$, an unflipped row by 0 (verified numerically to machine precision on all computed rows, §E.1). The **oracle-optimal set** is $X^*(\omega)=\arg\max_{x\in Q}V(x;\omega)$; the **regret** of $A$ is $r(A;\omega)=\max_xV(x;\omega)-V(x_A;\omega)\ge0$. Throughout, $\omega$ denotes the hidden labeling $(y_R, y_Q)$ and the dependence of $V$ on $\omega$ is through $y_x$ (which flip set) and $e$ (which signs).

**Reversal maps.** For $S\subseteq R$ let $\rho_S\omega$ be $\omega$ with $y_u$ negated for $u\in S$ (other labels unchanged). $\rho_R$ negates every reference label.

## §1 Lemmas

**Lemma 1 (invariance).** For every $S\subseteq R$: $I_n(\rho_S\omega)=I_n(\omega)$; hence every label-blind statistic, every fitted model, $p_n$, both flip sets $F_0(x),F_1(x)$, and every deterministic acquisition $A(I_n)$ take the same value in $\omega$ and $\rho_S\omega$. Since $x\in Q$ and $S\subseteq R$, $y_x$ is also unchanged, so the *realised* flip set $F_{y_x}(x)$ is the same in both worlds.

*Proof.* $I_n$ contains no label outside $D_n$ and $D_n\cap R=\emptyset$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (parity).** $e_u(\rho_S\omega)=-e_u(\omega)$ for $u\in S$ and $=e_u(\omega)$ otherwise, so
$$V(x;\rho_S\omega)=V(x;\omega)+\frac{2}{|R|}\sum_{u\in S\cap F_{y_x}(x)}e_u(\omega).\tag{1.1}$$
In particular $V(x;\rho_R\omega)=-V(x;\omega)$ for every $x\in Q$ simultaneously, and $V(x;\rho_S\omega)=V(x;\omega)$ whenever $S\cap F_{y_x}(x)=\emptyset$.

*Proof.* $\hat y_n(u)$ is $I_n$-measurable (Lemma 1) while $y_u$ is negated on $S$. Substitute into (0.1). $\square$

**Lemma 3 (what is even and what is odd).** Write $\omega=(y_Q,y_R)$. Every label-blind statistic is constant in $y_R$; every candidate-side statistic (including the true $y_x$, the realised flip set, and the model's own posterior expectation of $V$) is constant in $y_R$; $V(x;\omega)$ is an odd function of $e$ restricted to $F_{y_x}(x)$: it is *linear* in $e$ with coefficients $-\mathbb 1[u\in F_{y_x}(x)]/|R|$. Consequently, for any distribution $P$ on $y_R$ that is invariant under $\rho_{F_{y_x}(x)}$, $\mathbb E_P[V(x;\omega)\mid I_n,y_Q]=0$. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (the model's own expectation; Proposition 2 of the follow-up, restated on $R$).** Let $Q_n$ be the fitted model's joint posterior over hidden labels, with marginals $p_n(u)$ and one-step-updated marginals $p^{x,y}_{n+1}(u)$, and decisions $\hat y_n(u)=\mathbb 1[p_n(u)\ge\tfrac12]$. Then
$$\mathbb E_{Q_n}[V(x;\omega)\mid I_n]=\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}}Q_n(y_x=y)\,\frac{1}{|R|}\sum_{u\in F_y(x)}\bigl|2p^{x,y}_{n+1}(u)-1\bigr|\ \ge 0 .\tag{1.2}$$
*Proof.* On $F_y(x)$ the updated decision is the complement of $\hat y_n(u)$, so $Q_n(y_u=\hat y_n(u)\mid y_x=y)=\min(p^{x,y}_{n+1},1-p^{x,y}_{n+1})$ and $\mathbb E[e_u\mid y_x=y]=-|2p^{x,y}_{n+1}(u)-1|$. $\square$

(1.2) is the exact one-step expected accuracy gain under the model's own beliefs — the quantity XSUR (semantics S3) computes, here restricted to $R$. It is label-blind. Under $Q_n$ every candidate has non-negative expected value; the realised $V$ differs from (1.2) exactly by the miscalibration of $p_{n+1}$ at the flipped rows.

## §2 Theorems

### Theorem 1 (non-identifiability of the oracle-optimal query)

**Assumptions.**
- (A1) $R\cap D_n=\emptyset$ and the candidates lie in $Q=U\setminus(D_n\cup R)$ (transductive evaluation on unrevealed rows disjoint from the query pool — the thesis protocol).
- (A2) $I_n$ is as defined in §0: a function of the inputs, the revealed labels and the metadata only.
- (A3) $A$ is a deterministic function of $I_n$ (extension to randomised $A$ in Theorem 2(b)).
- (A4, non-degeneracy) For the true labeling $\omega$ there exist $x,x'\in Q$ with $V(x;\omega)\ne V(x';\omega)$.
- (A5, compatibility) The compatible set is closed under the reversal used: $\rho_R\omega\in\Omega_n$ (always true for $\Omega_n=\{0,1\}^{U\setminus D_n}$); for the local statement, $\rho_S\omega\in\Omega_n$ for the specific $S$ constructed below.

**Statement.** Under (A1)–(A5), for every label-blind acquisition $A$ there is a labeling $\omega'\in\{\omega,\rho_R\omega\}\subseteq\Omega_n$ with $A(I_n)\notin X^*(\omega')$. Both worlds have identical inputs, identical revealed labels, identical fitted M3 posterior, identical margins, identical flip sets $F_0(\cdot),F_1(\cdot)$, identical candidate labels $y_Q$, and identical values of every label-blind score, and their oracle rankings are exact reverses of each other: $X^*(\rho_R\omega)=\arg\min_xV(x;\omega)$.

Moreover (**local form**) for any two candidates $x^\star, x'$ with $D:=|R|\,(V(x^\star;\omega)-V(x';\omega))\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}$, there is a world $\omega''=\rho_S\omega$ that differs from $\omega$ in exactly $k=\lfloor D/2\rfloor+1$ reference labels, all inside $F_{y_{x^\star}}(x^\star)\,\triangle\,F_{y_{x'}}(x')$, with $V(x';\omega'')>V(x^\star;\omega'')$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 1, $x_A:=A(I_n)$ is the same in $\omega$ and $\rho_R\omega$. By Lemma 2, $V(\cdot;\rho_R\omega)=-V(\cdot;\omega)$, so $X^*(\rho_R\omega)=\arg\min V(\cdot;\omega)$. If $x_A\in X^*(\omega)\cap X^*(\rho_R\omega)$ then $V(x_A;\omega)=\max_xV=\min_xV$, contradicting (A4). Local form: write $F^\star=F_{y_{x^\star}}(x^\star)$, $F'=F_{y_{x'}}(x')$. By (1.1), negating $y_u$ for a *helpful* row of $x^\star$ outside $F'$ ($u\in F^\star\setminus F'$, $e_u=-1$) lowers $V(x^\star)$ by $2/|R|$ and leaves $V(x')$ unchanged; negating a *harmful* row of $x'$ outside $F^\star$ ($u\in F'\setminus F^\star$, $e_u=+1$) raises $V(x')$ by $2/|R|$ and leaves $V(x^\star)$ unchanged; rows in $F^\star\cap F'$ move both values equally. Let $a$ be the number of such available rows. Since $D=-\sum_{F^\star\setminus F'}e_u+\sum_{F'\setminus F^\star}e_u$ and every row of the symmetric difference contributes $+1$ if available and $-1$ otherwise, $D\le a$. Negating $k=\lfloor D/2\rfloor+1\le a$ available rows changes the integer gap to $D-2k<0$. $\square$

**Corollary 1 (any horizon; AULC; permanence).** Let $\pi$ be any adaptive label-blind policy that queries only in $Q$, and let $\mathrm{acc}_t(\pi;\omega)$ be the reference accuracy after $t$ queries. Then $\mathrm{acc}_t(\pi;\rho_R\omega)=1-\mathrm{acc}_t(\pi;\omega)$ for every $t$, so every gain functional $G(\pi;\omega)=\sum_tw_t(\mathrm{acc}_t-\mathrm{acc}_0)$ (in particular the AULC gain over any window) satisfies $G(\pi;\rho_R\omega)=-G(\pi;\omega)$, and the oracle-best path in $\omega$ is the oracle-worst path in $\rho_R\omega$. Because no query reveals a label in $R$, $I_{n+t}(\rho_R\omega)=I_{n+t}(\omega)$ for all $t$: the ambiguity is never resolved by further querying. The oracle headroom of the thesis (+0.095 q20 AULC 16–40 for the greedy test-label oracle) is therefore not a one-step blind spot but a quantity that is *invariant-undetermined* by the entire query process.

*Proof.* The decision sequence of $\pi$ is the same in both worlds (Lemma 1 applied at every $t$); the accuracy of any fixed decision vector under $\rho_R\omega$ is one minus its accuracy under $\omega$. $\square$

### Theorem 2 (regret lower bound and probability of choosing the oracle-best)

Let $\Delta(\omega):=\max_xV(x;\omega)-\min_xV(x;\omega)$ (the value range across candidates).

(a) **Minimax (deterministic).** For every label-blind $A$ and every $\omega$: $\max\{r(A;\omega),\,r(A;\rho_R\omega)\}\ \ge\ \Delta(\omega)/2$. Hence $\inf_A\sup_{\omega\in\Omega_n}r(A;\omega)\ \ge\ \tfrac12\sup_{\omega\in\Omega_n}\Delta(\omega)$.

(b) **Least-favourable prior (randomised rules included).** Under $\mu_\omega:=\tfrac12(\delta_\omega+\delta_{\rho_R\omega})$ every acquisition rule — deterministic, randomised, or the rule maximising the model's own expectation (1.2) — has expected value $\mathbb E_\mu V(x_A)=0$ and Bayes regret exactly $\Delta(\omega)/2$. No rule is better than uniform random selection under $\mu_\omega$.

(c) **Probability of the oracle-best.** If $P$ is any distribution on $y_R$ invariant under $\rho_S$ with $S=F(x_A)\triangle F(x')$ for some $x'$ whose realised flip set differs from that of $x_A$, then $P(x_A\text{ is the unique element of }X^*)\le\tfrac12$. Under $P_{\rm sym}$ ($e_u$ i.i.d. uniform on $\{\pm1\}$) and $m$ candidates with pairwise disjoint flip sets of equal size, $P_{\rm sym}(x_A\in X^*)=1/m$; in general $P_{\rm sym}(x_A\in X^*)\le\max_xP_{\rm sym}(x\in X^*)$, a label-blind computable *predictability ceiling* determined by the flip-set geometry alone.

(d) **Granularity.** $V$ takes values in $\{-|F|,\dots,|F|\}/|R|$ with steps of $2/|R|$; with $|R|=17$ one wrongly-decided reference row is worth $1/17=0.0588$ per query.

*Proof.* (a) $r(A;\omega)+r(A;\rho_R\omega)=\max V(\omega)-V(x_A;\omega)+\max(-V(\omega))+V(x_A;\omega)=\Delta(\omega)$. (b) $\mathbb E_\mu V(x;\cdot)=\tfrac12(V(x;\omega)-V(x;\omega))=0$ for every $x$, so every (possibly randomised) rule has expected value 0, and $\mathbb E_\mu\max_xV=\tfrac12(\max V(\omega)-\min V(\omega))$. (c) By (1.1), $V(x_A;\omega)-V(x';\omega)=-|R|^{-1}\bigl[\sum_{F(x_A)\setminus F(x')}e_u-\sum_{F(x')\setminus F(x_A)}e_u\bigr]$ is an odd function of $e$ on $S=F(x_A)\triangle F(x')$, so $\rho_S$ reverses its sign whenever it is non-zero. $\rho_S$ is a $P$-measure-preserving involution that maps $\{x_A\text{ unique argmax}\}\subseteq\{V(x_A)>V(x')\}$ injectively into $\{V(x_A)<V(x')\}\subseteq\{x_A\notin X^*\}$, whence $P(x_A\text{ unique argmax})\le P(x_A\notin X^*)\le 1-P(x_A\text{ unique argmax})$. Exchangeability gives $1/m$. (d) immediate from (0.1). $\square$

### Theorem 3 (minimal additional information and its required accuracy)

(a) **Candidate-side information does not break the ambiguity.** Revealing the true labels $y_x$ of *all* candidates (a perfect candidate-side oracle) determines every realised flip set $F_{y_x}(x)$ but leaves the invariance under $\rho_R$ intact: Theorems 1–2 hold verbatim with $I_n$ replaced by $(I_n,y_Q)$, and under any $\rho_S$-invariant prior the expected value of every candidate is still 0. Hence neither the query's own answer, nor any prediction of it, nor any measure of "how much the model would change" (influence, threshold shift, pool flips, disagreement) can identify the oracle-best query. The missing variable is reference-side: the sign vector $e$ on the union of flip sets. (Outside the symmetric case a candidate-side oracle can profit from an *asymmetric* flip-row prior; §E.5 measures this at one quarter of the headroom on the development data.)

(b) **One reference bit.** Under $P_{\rm sym}$, revealing $e_u$ (equivalently $y_u$) for a single $u\in R$ raises the best attainable expected value from $0$ to $\max_x\mathbb E[V(x)\mid e_u]=\tfrac{1}{|R|}\mathbb 1[e_u=-1\ \&\ \exists x:u\in F_{y_x}(x)]$; $k$ revealed rows at which the current decision is wrong and which some single candidate flips together are worth $k/|R|$.

(c) **Noisy reference-side proxy — exact recovery fraction.** Let $s_u=e_u\xi_u$ with $\xi_u$ i.i.d., $P(\xi_u=1)=q$, independent of $(I_n,\omega)$ (a "correctness proxy" at the reference rows: a low-fidelity simulation at $u$, an expert's regime call at $u$, a noisy or thresholded continuous output at $u$). Define the proxy value $\tilde V(x)=-|R|^{-1}\sum_{u\in F_{y_x}(x)}s_u$ and the proxy rule $x_s=\arg\max_x\tilde V(x)$ (with $y_x$ known, or replaced by the model's mixture over $y$). Under $P_{\rm sym}$:
$$\mathbb E[V(x)\mid s,y_Q]=(2q-1)\,\tilde V(x)\quad\text{for every }x,\qquad \mathbb E[V(x_s)]=(2q-1)\,\mathbb E_{\rm sym}\Bigl[\max_xV(x)\Bigr].\tag{2.1}$$
So the proxy rule attains exactly the fraction $2q-1$ of the oracle's expected headroom; it beats every label-blind rule (expected value 0) for every $q>\tfrac12$, and reaches a target $\tau$ iff $q\ \ge\ \tfrac12+\tau/(2\,\mathbb E_{\rm sym}[\max_xV])$.

(d) **The proxy must be new information.** If $s$ is a function of $I_n$ (any label-blind score, including the model's own confidence at $u$), then $s$ is $\rho_S$-invariant and $\mathbb E[e_u\mid s]=0$ under any $\rho_S$-invariant prior: its effective $q$ is $\tfrac12$. A reference-side proxy must be a *new measurement at the reference rows*.

*Proof.* (a) Lemma 3. (b) $\mathbb E[V(x)\mid e_u]=-e_u\mathbb 1[u\in F_{y_x}(x)]/|R|$ and the other terms vanish. (c) Under $P_{\rm sym}$, $e_u$ is uniform and independent of $\xi_u$, so $s_u$ is uniform and $\mathbb E[e_u\mid s_u]=(2q-1)s_u$; linearity of (0.1) gives the first identity; the Bayes rule given $s$ maximises $\tilde V$; since $(s_u)$ has the same law as $(e_u)$, $\max_x\tilde V$ has the same law as $\max_xV$. (d) Lemma 3. $\square$

**Remark (the flip-row prior is not a pessimistic device).** Theorems 2(b)–(c) and 3(c) use $P_{\rm sym}$, under which the current decision at a flipped row is right with probability $\tfrac12$. This is the least-favourable case, but it is also close to the empirical truth on this benchmark: flips land on the rows the model is least sure about, and there the model's decisions are near chance (§E.2). The minimax bound is therefore the operative bound, not a worst-case curiosity.

## §3 Two-world construction (explicit)

Fix the true state $\omega$ at any budget. **World $W_1$** $=\omega$. **World $W_2$** $=\rho_S\omega$ with

- *global twin*: $S=R$ (all 17 reference labels negated). Everything in $I_n$ is identical; $V_{W_2}(x)=-V_{W_1}(x)$ for all $x$; the oracle ranking is exactly reversed; the margin pick, the XSUR pick, the TV pick, the LAL-style cross-fitted pick and every other label-blind pick are the same in both worlds.
- *local twin*: $S\subseteq F(x^\star)\triangle F(x_m)$ of size $k=\lfloor D/2\rfloor+1$, where $x^\star$ is the oracle-best and $x_m$ the margin pick, chosen among the available rows so that the fitted model's own posterior odds of $W_2$ against $W_1$, $\prod_{u\in S}\bigl(\tfrac{\min(p_n(u),1-p_n(u))}{\max(p_n(u),1-p_n(u))}\bigr)^{e_u}$, are as large as possible. In $W_2$ the margin pick is at least as good as $x^\star$; $W_2$ differs from the truth in $k$ reference labels, at rows where M3 itself is uncertain.

The local twin answers the objection "the global twin is physically absurd": the model's own posterior assigns $W_2$ a probability comparable to $W_1$ (§E.3).

## §E Empirical instantiation on the old development data

Setting: the 80 states of the follow-up oracle dataset (20 runs × B ∈ {16, 24, 32, 40}, P1 prefixes of the committed M3-margin paths), $R$ = the 17 Fold-B1-q20 rows of the outer test fold, $|R|=17$. Candidates: every eligible candidate whose counterfactual q20 value is non-zero under at least one hypothetical label (`inventions_features.csv.gz`), plus the margin pick, plus a seeded 10 % sample of the remaining zero-valued candidates — 5,815 candidates, 11,630 exact M3 refits (both labels), each recording the flip set on $R$, the signs $e_u$, and $p_n(u)$, $p^{x,y}_{n+1}(u)$ at the flipped rows. Random-rule values use all 8,987 eligible candidates (the excluded ones have $V=0$).

**E.1 Identity (0.1).** $\max|V-(-|R|^{-1}\sum_{F}e_u)|=1.1\times10^{-16}$ over all 11,630 rows; agreement with `oracle_values.csv.gz` to $10^{-16}$. The oracle value is literally a signed count of flipped reference decisions: one row = 0.0588.

**E.2 The flip-row prior is close to the least-favourable one.** Under the true label, 28 % of candidates flip at least one reference decision (1 row: 1,316; 2: 186; 3: 78; 4: 39; 5: 10). Over the 2,128 flipped (candidate, row) pairs the current decision is wrong with frequency 0.584 (mean $e=-0.17$). Stratified by the model's confidence at the row before the query, $|2p_n(u)-1|$: ≤ 0.1 → mean $e=-0.009$ ($n=1{,}156$; exactly symmetric); 0.1–0.2 → −0.51 (318); 0.2–0.4 → −0.16 (150); 0.4–0.6 → +0.24 (68); > 0.6 → −0.41 (436). Where most flips occur the sign is a fair coin; where the model is confident and nevertheless flips, its decision was wrong 70 % of the time (over-confidence, not information). The model's own belief that a flip helps, $|2p_{n+1}-1|$, averages 0.167.

**E.3 Twin worlds.** In 58 of 80 states the oracle-best candidate strictly beats the margin pick (in the other 22 no candidate has positive value). For each of the 58, the local twin $W_2$ (Theorem 1, local form; §3) needs $k=1$ reference-label change in 36 states, $k=2$ in 20, $k=3$ in 2 (median symmetric difference of the two flip sets: 1 row). The fitted M3 posterior at those rows assigns $W_2$ a probability of 0.71 on average (median 0.68) against the truth $W_1$; in 84 % of the states the model considers the twin *more* likely than the truth (log-odds > 0). The model's exact posterior probability that the margin pick is at least as good as the oracle-best (independent Bernoulli($p_n$) on the symmetric difference, true candidate labels) is 0.64 on average, ≥ 0.5 in 76 % of the states. The reversal against the oracle-*worst* candidate needs $k\le 2$ in 51 of 58 states with model probability 0.65 for the twin. The global twin $\rho_R$ (all 17 labels negated) reverses all 80 states exactly and turns the mean reference accuracy 0.828 into 0.172. Files: `results/twin_worlds_pairs.csv`.

**E.4 Theorem 2 in numbers (per query, mean over 80 states; repeat-block s.d. in brackets).** Oracle-best $V_{\max}=+0.062$ [0.035]; oracle-worst $V_{\min}=-0.046$; range 0.107 (quartiles 0.059 / 0.118 / 0.118); minimax bound $\Delta/2=0.054$. Realised values: margin −0.004 [0.020]; model-Bayes rule maximising (1.2) (XSUR-S3 on $R$) −0.001 [0.028]; uniform random +0.003. Realised regrets: margin 0.066, model-Bayes 0.063, random 0.059 — all at or above the least-favourable-prior regret 0.054, i.e. the label-blind rules do no better than the symmetric-prior bound predicts. Probability of choosing an oracle-best candidate (states with $V_{\max}>0$): margin 0.075 [0.14], model-Bayes 0.15 [0.21], while the fraction of candidates tied at the maximum is 0.123 (the value of a uniformly random pick). Within-state Spearman with $V$: margin 0.01, model-Bayes 0.02. By budget the picture is the same (margin −0.012 / +0.006 / −0.012 / 0.000 at B16/24/32/40 against $V_{\max}$ 0.076 / 0.068 / 0.056 / 0.047).

**E.5 Theorem 3 in numbers.** (i) *Candidate-side information* (the true $y_x$ of every candidate, used through the model's own confidence at the realised flip set): +0.015 per query, oracle-best in 47.5 % of states, $\rho=0.30$ — one quarter of the headroom, obtained entirely from the confident-flip asymmetry of E.2 (under the symmetric prior this rule would be worth exactly 0; the empirical prior is 58/42, not 50/50). (ii) *Reference-side proxy* $s_u=e_u\xi_u$, accuracy $q$, 200 draws per state (`results/twin_worlds_proxy_curve.csv`):

| $q$ | 0.50 | 0.55 | 0.60 | 0.65 | 0.70 | 0.75 | 0.80 | 0.90 | 1.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathbb E\,V(x_s)$, $y_x$ known | 0.009 | 0.015 | 0.020 | 0.025 | 0.031 | 0.036 | 0.041 | 0.052 | 0.062 |
| $\mathbb E\,V(x_s)$, $y_x$ replaced by $p_n(x)$ | 0.002 | 0.005 | 0.008 | 0.010 | 0.014 | 0.015 | 0.018 | 0.023 | 0.027 |
| $P$(oracle-best), $y_x$ known | 0.28 | 0.32 | 0.35 | 0.40 | 0.44 | 0.48 | 0.52 | 0.62 | 0.73 |

The $y_x$-known row is linear in $2q-1$ to within Monte-Carlo error, $\mathbb E V(x_s)\approx v_0+(2q-1)(V_{\rm oracle}-v_0)$ with $v_0=0.009$ (the asymmetry baseline; Theorem 3(c) has $v_0=0$ under $P_{\rm sym}$) and reaches the oracle exactly at $q=1$. Required accuracies: to beat the margin pick (−0.004) any $q\ge0.5$ suffices once $y_x$ is known and $q\gtrsim0.55$ when it is not; +0.010 per query needs $q\approx0.52$ ($y_x$ known) or $q\approx0.65$ ($y_x$ unknown); half of the headroom (+0.031) needs $q=0.70$ ($y_x$ known) and is unreachable without $y_x$ even at $q=1$ (0.027). Both sides are needed for the full value: the candidate's own label selects the flip set, the reference signs select its sign.

**E.5b Independent checks.** Re-deriving the local twin from the raw flip sets with an arbitrary (not plausibility-ordered) choice of $k$ available rows reverses the (oracle-best, margin) ranking in 58/58 states. Simulating i.i.d. symmetric signs on the *real* flip-set geometry gives $\mathbb E\,V(x_s)$ = 0.0113 / 0.0313 / 0.0535 at $q$ = 0.6 / 0.8 / 1.0 against $(2q-1)\,\mathbb E_{\rm sym}[\max_xV]$ = 0.0108 / 0.0315 / 0.0535 (100 draws per state): identity (2.1) holds to Monte-Carlo error.

**E.6 What this says about the follow-up's negative results.** The 31 + 27 label-blind features, the LAL-style cross-fitted predictors, the XSUR family, TV and the three blue-sky inventions are all $\rho_S$-invariant; by Lemma 3 their only possible source of correlation with $V$ is the asymmetric part of the flip-row prior, which E.2 shows is confined to the rare confident flips. Their measured $\rho\le0.11$ and out-of-block $R^2<0$ are the expected outcome, not a failure of search.

## §K Literature (checked after the derivation)

- **No-free-lunch symmetry.** Theorem 1–2 are an instance of the averaging/symmetry argument behind Wolpert's no-free-lunch theorems for supervised learning: off-training-set performance, averaged over all target functions consistent with the training data, is algorithm-independent. Here the "off-training-set" rows are the reference rows $R$, the average is over the involution $\rho_R$ (or $\rho_S$), and the quantity is the one-step (or any-horizon) query value under 0-1 loss instead of generalisation error. The mechanism is classical; the object (query value on an unrevealed reference set) and the flip-set form (0.1) are specific to transductive active learning. [Wolpert 1996, *Neural Computation* 8(7); overview: no-free-lunch.org]
- **Active-learning lower bounds.** Dasgupta (2004/2005, "Analysis of a greedy active learning strategy"; "Coarse sample complexity bounds for active learning") gives worst-case lower bounds showing that for some hypotheses in a version space active learning cannot beat passive label complexity; Castro & Nowak (2007/2008, "Minimax bounds for active learning"), Raginsky & Rakhlin (2011) and Hanneke ("Theory of disagreement-based active learning", 2014; "Minimax analysis of active learning", JMLR 2015) give minimax label-complexity bounds. Those results bound *how many labels* are needed for a target error; the present result is about a different object — which single query is best *for a fixed reference set* — and is an exact non-identifiability rather than a rate.
- **Verifiable vs. unverifiable gains.** Balcan, Hanneke & Vaughan (2010, "The true sample complexity of active learning") separate the label complexity of *achieving* an error from that of *verifying* it. Corollary 1 is the transductive analogue one level up: the oracle path's gain is not only unverifiable from $I_{n+t}$, it is undetermined by it.
- **Version-space ambiguity.** Tosh & Dasgupta (2017, "Diameter-based active learning") measure the residual ambiguity of the version space by its diameter under the data distribution; the local twin ($k$ reference labels in the disagreement region) is the corresponding notion for a finite reference set: as long as the version space contains both $W_1$ and $W_2$ (Assumption A5), the query ranking is ambiguous.
- **Value of information / expected error reduction.** Roy & McCallum (2001, "Toward optimal active learning through sampling estimation of error reduction") and the MOCU line (Yoon, Dougherty & Qian 2013) compute (1.2) under the model's own posterior; the classical EVSI zero-flip principle (Raiffa & Schlaifer 1961; Howard 1966) is Lemma 4. None of these claim the realised value is identifiable; the distinction between (1.2) and $V$ is precisely $e$.
- **Learning to predict the realised gain.** Konyushkova, Sznitman & Fua (2017, "Learning active learning from data", NeurIPS) regress the realised error reduction on label-blind state features, trained across *other* datasets where the labels are known. The cross-fitted predictors of the follow-up (ridge/GBM, leave-repeat-block-out) are this construction applied within the benchmark; Theorem 3(d) explains why they fail here: any such regressor is a function of $I_n$ and is therefore $\rho_S$-invariant, so it can only succeed to the extent that the *prior* over $e$ at flip rows is asymmetric and learnable from features — which §E.2 shows it is not on this benchmark.
- **Clairvoyant/oracle baselines.** Test-label oracles are used in the empirical AL literature as upper bounds (the "hindsight" or "clairvoyant" strategy); I found no statement that the oracle ranking is non-identifiable from the label-blind state, but the argument is elementary and may exist in unpublished or lecture form. The novelty claim in §J is conservative accordingly.

Sources checked: Wolpert NFL overview (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_free_lunch_theorem, http://no-free-lunch.org/); Dasgupta greedy (https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~dasgupta/papers/greedy.pdf); Castro & Nowak (https://nowak.ece.wisc.edu/COLT07.pdf); Raginsky & Rakhlin (http://www.mit.edu/~rakhlin/papers/RagRak11nips.pdf); Balcan–Hanneke–Vaughan (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-010-5174-y); Tosh & Dasgupta (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/tosh17a.html); Konyushkova et al. (https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/8ca8da41fe1ebc8d3ca31dc14f5fc56c-Abstract.html); Roy & McCallum EER (https://www.academia.edu/158539/Toward_Optimal_Active_Learning_through_Sampling_Estimation_of_Error_Reduction).

## FINAL OUTPUT (A–J)

**A. Theorem / counterexample.** Theorem 1: under the thesis protocol (reference rows never queried), no deterministic label-blind acquisition $A(I_n)$ selects an oracle-optimal query uniformly over the labelings compatible with $I_n$; the counterexample is constructive — negating the hidden reference labels ($\rho_R$) leaves every element of $I_n$, every fitted posterior, every margin, every flip set and every label-blind score unchanged and reverses the oracle ranking exactly; a local twin differing in $k=\lfloor D/2\rfloor+1$ reference labels (empirically 1–3) reverses any chosen pair. Corollary 1 extends this to every horizon and to AULC gains: the +0.095 oracle headroom is a functional of labels that no query ever reveals.

**B. Assumptions.** (A1) reference set disjoint from the revealed set and from the candidates; (A2) $I_n$ = inputs, metadata, revealed labels and anything computed from them; (A3) deterministic $A$ (randomised rules covered by Theorem 2(b)); (A4) some pair of candidates has different oracle values (otherwise every rule is trivially optimal); (A5) the compatible set contains the twin — automatic for the unconstrained set, and for the local twin it requires only that the version space allows either label at 1–3 near-threshold reference rows. No assumption on the model class, kernel, calibration or acquisition family.

**C. Proof sketch.** Identity (0.1): $V(x)=-|R|^{-1}\sum_{u\in F_{y_x}(x)}e_u$ with $I_n$-measurable flip sets and non-measurable signs (verified to $10^{-16}$). Lemma 1: $I_n$ is invariant under any reversal of reference labels. Lemma 2: $V$ is odd in $e$ on the flip set, so $\rho_R$ negates every $V(x)$ simultaneously. A deterministic $A$ picks the same $x_A$ in both worlds; it cannot be the argmax and the argmin unless $V$ is constant. The local form is a counting argument on the symmetric difference of two flip sets. Regret: $r(A;\omega)+r(A;\rho_R\omega)=\Delta(\omega)$. Proxy: under the symmetric prior $\mathbb E[e_u\mid s_u]=(2q-1)s_u$ and linearity of (0.1).

**D. Two-world construction.** $W_1$ = the truth; $W_2=\rho_S W_1$. Global: $S=R$. Local: $S$ = the $k$ most model-plausible rows among the helpful rows of $x^\star$ outside $F(x_m)$ and the harmful rows of $x_m$ outside $F(x^\star)$. Same inputs, same revealed labels, same fitted M3 posterior and margins, same flip sets, same candidate labels, same value of every label-blind score; in $W_2$ the margin pick is at least as good as the oracle-best of $W_1$.

**E. Empirical instantiation (80 old-data states, 11,630 exact refits).** In 58/80 states the oracle-best beats margin; the reversing twin needs 1 (36 states), 2 (20) or 3 (2) reference-label changes; M3's own posterior gives the twin probability 0.71 on average against the truth and prefers the twin in 84 % of states; the model's posterior probability that margin is at least as good as the oracle-best is 0.64. At the rows where flips land the current decision is wrong with frequency 0.584 overall and 0.504 where $|2p_n-1|\le0.1$ (1,156 of 2,128 flip rows): the least-favourable symmetric prior is approximately the empirical truth. Realised per-query values: oracle +0.062, margin −0.004, model-Bayes (XSUR-S3 on $R$) −0.001, random +0.003; $P$(oracle-best) 0.075 / 0.15 / 0.12.

**F. Lower bound.** Theorem 2: minimax regret $\ge\tfrac12\sup_\omega\Delta(\omega)$; under the least-favourable pair prior every rule — deterministic, randomised, or model-Bayes — has expected value 0 and regret exactly $\Delta/2$ (= 0.054 per query on the development states; realised label-blind regrets 0.059–0.066); $P(\text{oracle-best})\le\tfrac12$ under any reversal-invariant prior and $=1/m$ for $m$ exchangeable candidates; one wrong reference row is worth $1/|R|=0.059$.

**G. Minimal extra information.** Theorem 3: the ambiguity is broken only by information about the reference-side signs $e_u$ (equivalently the labels $y_u$) at the rows the candidates flip; candidate-side information (the query's own answer, influence, disagreement, threshold shift) does not break it; any proxy that is a function of $I_n$ has effective accuracy $\tfrac12$. A reference-side proxy of accuracy $q$ recovers exactly the fraction $2q-1$ of the oracle's expected headroom under the symmetric prior (empirically $0.009+(2q-1)\times0.053$ with the candidate label known; at most 0.027 even at $q=1$ without it). To beat margin: any $q>\tfrac12$ ($q\gtrsim0.55$ without the candidate label); +0.010 per query: $q\approx0.52$ / $0.65$; half the headroom: $q=0.70$ with the candidate label, unreachable without it. The signal must be a new measurement at the reference rows — a low-fidelity simulation, an expert regime call, or the continuous depth at $u$ — evaluated where the flips land, i.e. at near-threshold rows; inside the frozen protocol those rows are the blinded endpoint, so the only legitimate form is a pool-wide low-fidelity proxy available before any label (multi-fidelity), and it must be ≥ 55–65 % accurate *on near-threshold rows* to be worth anything.

**H. Meaning for the thesis.** The saturation of label-blind acquisition at the margin level is not a shortfall of the candidates tried in Rounds 1–3; it is the structural situation of transductive 0-1 evaluation on an unrevealed reference set. The oracle headroom measures the *value of the reference labels*, not an unexploited property of the inputs. Consequently (i) the external protocol needs no change — no label-blind arm can be expected to approach the oracle, and the frozen challenger M3+TV is tested for a small effect for the right reason; (ii) P9 (oracle gain ≥ +0.05) is a prediction about label-value, and its confirmation on the new pool would confirm the theorem's premise (asymmetric-but-near-symmetric flip-row prior), not a missed opportunity; (iii) the only way to move the early-window endpoint by a large amount is multi-fidelity information at near-threshold rows, which is a different experimental design, not a different acquisition rule.

**I. Exact safe claim.** "For transductive active learning evaluated by 0-1 accuracy on a fixed set of unrevealed reference rows, the one-step (and any-horizon) oracle value of a query is a signed count of the reference decisions the query would flip, $V(x)=-|R|^{-1}\sum_{u\in F_{y_x}(x)}e_u$, whose flip sets are computable before the query and whose signs are not. Negating the hidden reference labels leaves all label-blind information unchanged and reverses every oracle value, so no deterministic label-blind acquisition selects an oracle-optimal query uniformly over labelings compatible with the data, the minimax regret is at least half the value range, and under the least-favourable prior every acquisition rule — including the model's own expected-error-reduction rule — is equivalent to random selection. On the development data the reversing twin differs from the truth in one to three reference labels that the fitted model itself considers more likely than the truth, and the flip-row prior is near-symmetric, so the bound is operative. Breaking the ambiguity requires new information about the reference labels at near-threshold rows; a proxy of accuracy $q$ recovers the fraction $2q-1$ of the headroom. The result is an instance of the no-free-lunch symmetry argument applied to query value; its content is the flip-set identity, the local (few-label) twin, and the quantitative proxy threshold, not the symmetry argument itself."

**J. Thesis-worthiness.** Yes, as a short theoretical section closing the acquisition chapter: it converts three rounds of negative results into a stated, proved and empirically instantiated non-identifiability with explicit constants ($1/|R|$ per row, $\Delta/2$, $2q-1$). Its novelty is modest and should be described as such — the mechanism is Wolpert's; the transductive flip-set form, the model-plausible local twin and the proxy-accuracy threshold are the specific contributions, and the claim that the empirical flip-row prior is near-symmetric is what makes the bound bite on this benchmark. It should not be presented as a general impossibility theorem for active learning (it says nothing about inductive generalisation error, about noisy labels, or about targets other than 0-1 accuracy on a fixed reference set), and it should be paired with the positive statement that the oracle's +0.095 is real and would be attainable with a multi-fidelity reference-side signal of stated accuracy.
